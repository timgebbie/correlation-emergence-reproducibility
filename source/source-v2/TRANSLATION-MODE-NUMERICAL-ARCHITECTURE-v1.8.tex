\subsection{Regularised analytical source and translation-mode numerics}
\label{app:translation-mode-numerics}

The regularised coupling used in the analytical reduction and the source used
in the production numerical experiment have different roles.  The analytical
construction introduces a bounded smooth selector and uses its weak projection
onto the reaction-front displacement mode.  It is an approximating closure
that makes the small-spread calculation tractable; it is not asserted to be a
pointwise representation of the complete finite-grid recurrence.

For the numerical experiment we instead use the current receiving-book
translation mode
\begin{equation}
  v_j(x,u)=-\partial_x\varphi^{(j)}(x,u),
  \qquad
  \ell_T^{(j,k)}(x,u)
  =-\kappa_{jk}z_{jk}(u)v_j(x,u),
  \qquad
  z_{jk}=p_j-p_k.
  \label{eq:numerical-translation-source}
\end{equation}
If $\psi_j$ is the adjoint displacement mode and
$N_j=\langle\psi_j,v_j\rangle$, then
\begin{equation}
  \dot p_j
  =\frac{\langle\psi_j,\ell_T^{(j,k)}\rangle}{N_j}
  =-\kappa_{jk}(p_j-p_k).
  \label{eq:numerical-projected-boundary-law}
\end{equation}
Thus $\kappa_{jk}$ is the ordered boundary-response rate itself rather than a
source amplitude that still requires a separately assumed amplitude-to-rate
map.

No independent selector width or mesoscale boundary thickness is introduced
in Equation~\eqref{eq:numerical-translation-source}.  The reaction front is
nevertheless not treated as infinitely thin: its finite grid-resolved profile
is contained in the current density $\varphi^{(j)}$ and enters through its
spatial derivative.  The effective numerical thickness is therefore inherited
from the resolved front generated by the diffusion, source, grid, boundary
conditions and path history.  It is not an additional coupling parameter.

The regularised analytical source and the translation-mode numerical source
are consequently compared only after projection onto the front-displacement
mode.  They are not pointwise identical.  The analytical boundary law remains
a conditional reduced result, while the translation-mode implementation is a
direct numerical realisation of that reduced response.  The earlier
regularised finite-grid implementation is retained separately as a historical
comparator and is not used as the claim-bearing production coupling.
