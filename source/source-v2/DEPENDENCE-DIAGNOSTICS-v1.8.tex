\subsection{Mid-price and trade-sign dependence across time layers}
\label{app:dependence-diagnostics}

The numerical dependence experiment first constructs a complete uniform
operational event tape.  A market event at index $n_r$ changes the current
density immediately before the ordinary fixed-step update from $n_r-1$ to
$n_r$.  Neither a stochastic waiting time nor a calendar clock enters this
recurrence.  Once both price paths $p_j(u_n)$ are complete, book-specific
previous-refresh subordination supplies the calendar image
\begin{equation}
  P_j(t)=p_j\!\left(U_j(t)\right),
  \qquad
  U_j(t)=\max\{u_{j,m}:t_{j,m}\leq t\}.
\end{equation}
No calendar interpolation and no state update on the resulting nonuniform
refresh times are used.

For a fixed sampling interval $h$, the price estimand is the autocorrelation
of log-mid increments
\begin{equation}
  r_{j,k}=P_j(kh)-P_j((k-1)h),
  \qquad
  C^{p}_{j}(\ell)=\operatorname{Corr}(r_{j,k},r_{j,k-\ell}).
\end{equation}
The corresponding operational statistic replaces $P_j$ by $p_j$ on the
uniform grid.  Price-level autocorrelation is deliberately omitted: it is not
the stationary price-change estimand used here.

The same fully filled market-event records are measured under three sign
conventions: declared ground-truth aggressor side, execution-proxy position
relative to the pre-event midpoint, and a legacy tick rule whose first value
is zero and whose zero ticks carry the previous nonzero sign.  Passive limit
orders are nontrades and do not enter these series.  Same-book event-lag sign
autocorrelation and calendar-binned signed-flow autocorrelation are reported
separately.  The latter first maps events through the appropriate book clock,
forms subordinated cumulative signed count, and then differences its
five-second calendar samples.  Thus an event lag is not relabelled as a
calendar lag.

The declared signs follow a finite Markov persistence fixture chosen to test
the estimators.  Persistence is an exogenous input, not endogenous long memory
generated by the order-book dynamics.  Likewise, agreement of the midpoint
rule with declared side follows from the one-front event construction, and the
tick rule acts on a consumed-grid execution-price proxy rather than an observed
transaction record.  The diagnostics are therefore not an empirical
calibration of sign persistence or trade classification.

This extension inherits the current receiving-front translation-mode source
and its resolved finite density profile.  It introduces no independent
regularisation width, mesoscale boundary thickness, new coupling rate or
theoretical Epps curve.  The bounded analytical source in the accepted paper
remains a weak-moment closure, while the numerical source is compared at the
projected front-displacement law as described in
Appendix~\ref{app:translation-mode-numerical-source}.
