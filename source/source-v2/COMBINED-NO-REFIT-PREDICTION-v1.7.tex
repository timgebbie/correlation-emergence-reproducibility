\section*{Combined clock and corrected-coupling no-refit prediction}

The accepted clock-only and corrected coupling-only components are combined
without changing either book clock rate, either ordered coupling rate or the
thick-boundary covariance normalization.  The numerical order of operations is
now explicit: both order-density books are first evolved to completion on one
fixed uniform operational-time grid, and independent book-specific
previous-refresh clocks are applied only afterwards.  No realised waiting time
enters the state recurrence, and no calendar-time interpolation is used.

For equal book refresh rates $\lambda=0.1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and total
coupling rate $\kappa=0.025\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, the manuscript's leading-order
separability approximation is
\[
  R_{\mathrm{prod}}(\Delta)
  =F(\lambda\Delta)F(\kappa\Delta),
  \qquad
  F(x)=1-\frac{1-e^{-x}}{x}.
\]
The component-only simulation curves also define a separately frozen product.
Neither product is fitted to the combined simulation.

The product is not an exact finite-sample estimator identity.  To distinguish
an estimator effect from an implementation defect, the reduced symmetric
coupled process is evaluated conditionally on the exact operational indices
selected by the realised previous-refresh clocks.  For the two selected
operational intervals $[a,b]$ and $[c,d]$, define
\[
  O=\max\{0,\min(b,d)-\max(a,c)\},
\]
and
\[
  G=e^{-\kappa|b-d|}-e^{-\kappa|b-c|}
    -e^{-\kappa|a-d|}+e^{-\kappa|a-c|}.
\]
With unit pair-centre variance rate, the exact conditional cross-covariance is
\[
  C(a,b,c,d)=O-\frac{G}{2\kappa},
\]
while a same-book interval of length $L$ has variance
\[
  V(L)=L+\frac{1-e^{-\kappa L}}{\kappa}.
\]
Summing these quantities over exactly the windows used by the numerical
estimator gives an exact estimator-aware reference for normalized covariance and return
correlation.  This reference owns no random-number generator, clock,
interpolation or operational dynamics.

The reduced simulation recovers this exact conditional reference: normalized
covariance RMSE is $0.008530$, return-correlation RMSE is $0.002296$, both
standardized RMSE values are below $0.41$, and both comparisons have pointwise
normal 95\% coverage $1.0$.  The leading-order product nevertheless differs
from the exact conditional reduced covariance by RMSE $0.037391$, above the
registered $0.03$ threshold.  This is a qualified scientific nonconformity:
it measures intrinsic estimator-level nonseparability and does not invalidate
the operational-time simulation or post-dynamics subordination.

Using the same clock rule with the accepted thick-boundary implementation, the
boundary-specific normalized-covariance residual relative to the exact reduced
same-clock reference has RMSE $0.039719$.  Its standardized RMSE is $0.454980$
and its coverage is $1.0$; the corresponding return-correlation residual has
RMSE $0.014858$.  The total thick-boundary residual relative to the analytical
product has RMSE $0.066963$, and the residual relative to the accepted
component product has RMSE $0.056040$.  The plateau proxy shifts by $0.077190$
relative to its registered tolerance of $0.05$.

These results refine rather than contradict the manuscript.  Appendix C
already labels the product a leading-order separability approximation and
states that it need not hold when clock overlap and coupling transmission are
materially dependent.  The experiment supplies the missing quantitative
decomposition: one residual is present even in the exact reduced
estimator-aware calculation, and a second is attributable to the thick
reaction-boundary representation.  The product remains a useful mechanism
summary, but it must not be described as an exact numerical consequence of
independent observation clocks and finite coupling.
