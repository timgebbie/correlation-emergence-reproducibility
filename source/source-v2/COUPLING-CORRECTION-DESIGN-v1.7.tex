\section*{Translation-mode coupling correction}

Let the directed spread and the current receiving-book translation mode be
\[
  z_{jk}=p^{(j)}-p^{(k)},
  \qquad
  v_j(x,u)=-\partial_x\varphi^{(j)}(x,u).
\]
The accepted reaction-front reduction uses an adjoint response mode $\psi_j$
and normalization
\[
  N_j=\langle\psi_j,v_j\rangle\ne0,
  \qquad
  \dot p_j
  =\frac{\langle\psi_j,\ell^{(j,k)}\rangle}{N_j}.
\]
The minimal source that realizes the registered ordered-pair response is
therefore
\[
  \ell_T^{(j,k)}(x,u)
  =-\kappa_{jk}z_{jk}(u)v_j(x,u)
  =\kappa_{jk}z_{jk}(u)\partial_x\varphi^{(j)}(x,u).
\]
Indeed,
\[
  \dot p_j
  =\frac{\langle\psi_j,-\kappa_{jk}z_{jk}v_j\rangle}
  {\langle\psi_j,v_j\rangle}
  =-\kappa_{jk}z_{jk}.
\]
For two reciprocal ordered pairs,
\[
  \dot z
  =\dot p_1-\dot p_2
  =-(\kappa_{12}+\kappa_{21})z.
\]
Thus the sign is determined by the receiving-book convention: if $p^{(j)}$ is
above $p^{(k)}$, then $z_{jk}>0$ and book $j$ moves down.

The thickness of this coupling is the thickness of the current reaction front
itself.  The mode is computed from the immutable pre-step density on the
accepted uniform price grid.  No fixed-$\varepsilon$ selector enters the
claim-bearing leading response.  The accepted smooth side-selected source is
retained as a comparator, while a hard-side or finite-spatial-width residual is
deferred.  Any such residual must first be made orthogonal to the adjoint
translation response so that it cannot alter $\kappa_{jk}$.

For the symmetric target,
\[
  \kappa_{12}=\kappa_{21}=1.25,
  \qquad
  \kappa=2.5
\]
per model-time unit. With $\Delta u=0.005$, the registered explicit spread
factor is $1-\kappa\Delta u=0.9875$. A deterministic one-step probe through the
existing external-source entry recovers rates from $2.498338$ to $2.499672$
over signed spreads with maximum relative error $0.000665$, zero pair-centre
drift to numerical precision and unique interior boundaries.

This calculation selects an implementation contract; it is not yet the
v1.7.7 production coupling or a stochastic Epps-curve validation.
